http://info.cern.ch/hypertext/WWW/TheProject.html

Taken 2026-09-03 with `curl`. The server answered HTTP/1.1 200; the bytes here
are the body exactly as it sent them, unedited.

Why this page. It is the first web page, restored and published by CERN as a
historical record, so freezing it needs nobody's leave and anybody can re-derive
it. And it is the purest possible test of the work queue item 171 had just
finished: **it has no style sheet at all.** Every pixel of it comes from the
user-agent sheet, which until item 171 said what elements are and nothing about
what they look like.

It is also 1991 markup, which is a second thing to test entirely: uppercase tag
names, an unclosed `<P>`, `<DT>` and `<DD>` closed by the next one starting, and
a `<HEADER>` element that meant `<HEAD>` and no longer does.

## What it found

**Links had no colour.** The sheet said `text-decoration: underline` and nothing
else, so on a page that is almost nothing but links — this one — the whole thing
rendered as an undifferentiated wall of black text with no way to see what could
be followed. `a:any-link` has a colour now. It is `:any-link` rather than `a`
because an `<a>` without an `href` is an anchor, not a link, and this page is
full of them.

**`text-decoration: underline` is declared and never painted.** It has been in
the user-agent sheet all along and produces no paint operation at all. Nothing
in the alo cases underlines anything, so nothing noticed. Queue item 173.

**A wrapped inline reports one rectangle covering all of its lines.** `link
"Frequently Asked Questions"` comes back as 778×37 starting at the left margin,
because it wraps and the tree reports the union of its fragments. Nothing acts
on that — ADR 0002 means no verb takes a coordinate — but it decides whether a
node counts as offscreen, and it is what a person reading the tree sees. Queue
item 174.

## What it did not find, which is the point of running it

The `<DL>` indents by forty pixels, the `<H1>` is 2em with its margins, the
paragraph has its own, and the four things the parser could not make sense of
are all in `issues.txt` rather than silently dropped. `<HEADER>` is parsed as
the HTML5 `<header>` element and comes back as a `banner` landmark of zero
height — which is the correct modern reading of markup written before either
existed, and is the kind of thing that is only funny once you can see it.
